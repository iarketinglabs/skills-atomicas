# YouTube Content Strategy

Turn a business goal and audience context into a practical, testable YouTube plan. Optimize for audience value and business relevance rather than chasing a single metric or a presumed algorithm trick.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent channel analytics, audience research, competitor performance, or platform policy.
- State assumptions clearly and ask only for information that changes the recommendation.
- Treat current algorithm behavior, monetization rules, product features, and synthetic-content disclosure as time-sensitive. Verify them with official YouTube sources when the recommendation depends on them.
- Do not promise views, revenue, subscribers, or distribution.
- Keep the strategy independent of a specific company, agency, creator, software stack, or content-management tool.

## Start with the decision context

Collect or infer the following. If key inputs are missing, provide a provisional plan with an `Assumptions and unknowns` section, then ask the smallest number of follow-up questions needed.

1. Goal: awareness, demand generation, revenue, education, community, support, credibility, or another stated outcome.
2. Audience: primary viewer, their job or situation, problem, language, level of awareness, and viewing intent.
3. Offer and positioning: what the organization does, why it is credible, and the distinctive point of view it can sustain.
4. Operating constraints: people on camera, production capacity, budget range, approval requirements, publishing window, and reusable material.
5. Evidence: existing channel data, customer questions, sales/support calls, search themes, competitor examples, or research. Label each input as evidence or assumption.
6. Conversion path: desired next action and how it can be measured without forcing every video into a sales pitch.

## Choose the strategic shape

### 1. Match the channel to the audience and business model

Classify the operating context as B2B, B2C, nonprofit/education, creator-led, or hybrid. This is a starting lens, not a rule:

- In B2B, prioritize specific problems, decision journeys, expertise, proof, and qualified actions. Measure business contribution alongside audience signals.
- In B2C, prioritize relevance, emotional resonance, product discovery, community, and shareability. Connect attention to a clear but proportionate next step.
- In hybrid contexts, specify which audience and outcome each content line serves; do not blend incompatible calls to action in one video merely to serve every goal.

Read `references/strategy-frameworks.md` when choosing content portfolio, audience scope, or content sourcing.

### 2. Build a balanced portfolio

Use Hero-Hub-Help as a menu, not a fixed ratio:

- **Hero:** a distinctive moment, launch, investigation, event, or collaboration that can introduce the channel to new audiences.
- **Hub:** recurring series that gives returning viewers a reason to come back.
- **Help:** searchable, durable answers to concrete questions or jobs to be done.

Add a source-of-production decision for each line: original work, collaboration, or ethically cleared community/customer material. Choose formats based on their job, not on a universal cadence.

### 3. Design each concept before scripting

For every proposed video, define:

- Viewer segment: core, adjacent/casual, or new-to-category.
- Viewer tension: a question, obstacle, desire, misconception, or decision.
- Specific promise: what changes for the viewer by the end.
- Evidence or demonstration: what makes the promise credible.
- Format and duration range: choose based on the idea and viewer intent, not a universal duration rule.
- Next action: a relevant video, playlist, resource, conversation, purchase path, or no CTA when the experience should stand alone.

Do not recommend a concept if it serves no identifiable viewer need and no strategic role.

## Package and produce for a fulfilled promise

Read `references/production-and-retention.md` when designing a video, script, title, thumbnail, or retention diagnosis.

1. Create a title and thumbnail as one promise. They should generate relevant curiosity without concealing the subject or overstating the outcome.
2. Open by establishing proof, value, structure, and stakes in the viewer's language. Remove greetings, context, or self-promotion that delays the promised value unless they are part of the story.
3. Structure the body around progress: demonstrate, explain, contrast, or resolve in a sequence the viewer can follow.
4. Deliver meaningful takeaways throughout. Change the visual or narrative mode when it helps attention, not as a substitute for substance.
5. Close with a next step that matches the viewer's current intent. Prefer a relevant next video or playlist when the goal is deeper viewing.
6. Treat Shorts and long-form as distinct format choices with shared editorial coherence. Repurpose only when the short-form version has its own complete promise.

## Measure, learn, and govern

Read `references/measurement-and-governance.md` when selecting metrics, designing tests, using AI-assisted media, or commenting on current YouTube rules.

1. Define one primary outcome per content line, then select leading indicators and a decision threshold. Examples: qualified inquiries, repeat viewers, completion of an educational sequence, relevant clicks, or customer questions answered.
2. Review packaging, opening, progression, satisfaction signals, and conversion path separately. Avoid diagnosing every result as a thumbnail issue or an algorithm issue.
3. Test one meaningful variable at a time where practical: audience premise, topic angle, packaging, opening, format, or CTA.
4. Record the hypothesis, change, result, confidence, and next decision. Preserve losing tests when they reveal an audience constraint.
5. When realistic synthetic or altered media is involved, confirm the current disclosure and content policies with official sources, document what was used, and protect authenticity, rights, consent, and source accuracy.

## Anti-patterns

- Do not prescribe a universal posting frequency, video duration, CTR target, retention threshold, or content mix.
- Do not equate short-form volume with long-form strategy, or reach with business value.
- Do not use clickbait that the video cannot fulfill.
- Do not repeat information-dense cuts, motion, or effects if they make comprehension harder.
- Do not present outdated platform claims as permanent facts.
- Do not treat AI assistance as either an automatic advantage or an automatic penalty; evaluate originality, viewer value, rights, disclosure, and policy compliance.

# Measurement, Experiments, and Governance

Use this reference for reporting, audits, experiments, AI-assisted production, or platform-related recommendations.

## Build a measurement chain

Start from a desired outcome, then select evidence at each stage.

| Layer | Question | Example signals |
|---|---|---|
| Business or mission outcome | What change matters outside YouTube? | Qualified conversations, sales influence, successful learning, support deflection, donations, repeat purchase |
| Viewer value | Did the intended viewer find the content worth their time? | Return behavior, completion patterns, qualitative feedback, saves, relevant comments, survey results |
| Discovery and packaging | Did the right people choose the video? | Impressions by audience, click behavior, traffic source, search query alignment |
| Video experience | Did the promise and progression hold attention? | Early drop-off, relative retention, chapter behavior, next-video viewing |
| Conversion path | Did the next step fit the moment? | Relevant end-screen clicks, playlist continuation, resource usage, attributable visits |

Do not use one metric as a universal score. Compare performance to a meaningful baseline: similar format, viewer intent, topic type, or prior experiment.

## Experiment log

Use a simple log to avoid hindsight bias:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | The expected cause-and-effect statement |
| Audience and content line | Who and what the test concerns |
| Variable changed | One meaningful change where practical |
| Baseline | The comparison item or historical range |
| Signals | Primary and secondary measures |
| Review window | When enough evidence is expected |
| Result | Observed outcome and confidence limits |
| Decision | Keep, revise, stop, or test a new hypothesis |

An experiment can be inconclusive. Record this rather than assigning a false causal explanation.

## Audit discipline

For an audit, distinguish three levels:

- **Observed evidence:** visible channel material or supplied analytics.
- **Inference:** a plausible explanation that requires testing or more data.
- **Recommendation:** a proposed change connected to an observation or hypothesis.

Do not diagnose an algorithmic penalty from ordinary variance, limited data, or a single underperforming video.

## AI, rights, and policy checks

Artificial intelligence can assist with research, ideation, transcription, editing, imagery, sound, translation, and production. Its use does not replace accountability for accuracy, originality, rights, consent, and viewer trust.

When a request relies on current YouTube policy or product behavior:

1. Check official YouTube Help, Creator resources, or policy documentation at the time of the decision.
2. Separate official requirements from market commentary and creator anecdotes.
3. Confirm disclosure requirements for realistic altered or synthetic material.
4. Check that people, voices, likenesses, customer material, music, footage, and claims have appropriate permission and context.
5. Avoid repetitive or low-value automation. Preserve meaningful editorial judgment and a viewer-facing reason to watch.

If live verification is unavailable, state that current policy details were not verified and avoid giving definitive compliance advice.

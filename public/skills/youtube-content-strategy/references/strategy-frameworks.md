# Strategy Frameworks for YouTube

Use this reference after the channel objective and audience are known. Frameworks are decision aids; do not turn them into fixed ratios or mandatory labels.

## Hero-Hub-Help portfolio

| Role | Viewer job | Typical use | Planning question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | Discover a strong idea, moment, or perspective | Campaigns, launches, collaborations, original investigations, tentpole events | What could earn attention beyond the existing audience while remaining true to the channel? |
| Hub | Return for a familiar value exchange | Recurring series, expert formats, regular episodes, themed franchises | Why would a viewer recognize and choose the next installment? |
| Help | Solve a concrete, durable problem | Tutorials, explainers, comparisons, FAQs, problem diagnosis | What exact question or job will this answer better than available alternatives? |

Build a portfolio that reflects resources and the objective. A small expert channel may need mostly Help and Hub; an established consumer brand may occasionally fund Hero. State why the mix fits instead of copying a percentage.

## Create-Collaborate-Curate

Use this model to decide who can credibly make the content.

- **Create:** first-party material, expertise, demonstrations, original reporting, or brand stories.
- **Collaborate:** co-created work with a specialist, customer, creator, partner, or community member whose contribution changes the viewer value.
- **Curate:** material from customers or the community, used only with permission, context, rights clearance, and truthful representation.

Collaboration is not valuable solely because another person has an audience. Identify the distinct expertise, perspective, access, or proof the collaborator contributes.

## Audience scope: core, adjacent, and new

For each idea, state its primary audience.

- **Core:** people already invested in the category, creator, or problem.
- **Adjacent:** people with a neighboring interest who can understand the premise with limited context.
- **New:** people unfamiliar with the category who need a more accessible entry point.

Do not force one video to serve all three. A portfolio can contain all three, with packaging and explanation depth adapted accordingly.

## B2B and B2C as planning lenses

| Dimension | B2B tendency | B2C tendency | Hybrid decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Viewer intent | Resolve an operational, professional, or purchasing problem | Discover, enjoy, identify, compare, or act | Name the viewer's current job explicitly |
| Proof | Expertise, process, data, case evidence, implementation detail | Product experience, relevance, cultural fit, customer story, utility | Select proof that the named viewer will trust |
| Conversion | Qualified conversation, trial, demo, resource, sales enablement | Product discovery, purchase, loyalty, community action | Avoid a CTA that conflicts with the video's promise |
| Success | Pipeline contribution, qualified demand, educational progress, trust | Reach quality, engagement, product discovery, repeat affinity | Pair an audience signal with a business signal |

These are tendencies, not format restrictions. A B2B video can be entertaining, and a B2C video can be instructional.

## Concept scorecard

Use a lightweight qualitative scorecard to rank ideas. Score each as low, medium, or high, with one sentence of evidence.

1. Audience demand or tension
2. Distinctive point of view or access
3. Proof available for the claim
4. Packaging potential
5. Strategic role in the portfolio
6. Production feasibility
7. Relevant conversion or next-viewing path

Prioritize ideas with a coherent case across the dimensions. A high score in one dimension cannot compensate for a missing viewer need or unsupported promise.

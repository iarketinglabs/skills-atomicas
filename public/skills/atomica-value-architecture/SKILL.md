# Atomica Value Architecture

Use this skill to apply Atomica's positioning and value architecture in strategy, copy, offers, sales assets, landing pages, outreach, pitch materials, and product messaging.

Atomica's core frame:

> Atomica is not "more AI for marketing." Atomica is a Marketing Transformation Partner with AI: strategy, systems, implementation, training, and adoption until AI becomes operational.

## Workflow

1. Identify the motion: B2B, B2C, or B2C-to-B2B bridge.
2. Select the ICP and primary metric before writing any claim.
3. Anchor the message in the relevant job-to-be-done, pain, and buying trigger.
4. Express value through efficiency, quality, and speed.
5. Choose the right proof format: quick win, POC, caselet, ROI calculation, testimonial, training outcome, or operational metric.
6. Route the offer:
   - B2B readiness/transformation when there is no clear champion, access, or operating system.
   - B2B execution/quick wins when there is a sponsor and a 14-30 day proof is feasible.
   - B2C mentorship/community when the buyer is an individual seeking practical capability.
7. Write the competitive narrative against inaction, traditional agencies/courses, and self-directed AI adoption without attacking people or tools.

## Output Standards

When producing Atomica messaging, include:

- ICP or audience named explicitly.
- Main pain or job-to-be-done.
- One clear 7-second value anchor.
- Proof points mapped to efficiency, quality, and speed when relevant.
- Primary metric or success signal.
- Offer route or next step.
- Guardrail-aware wording with no hype claims.

When reviewing existing messaging, diagnose:

- Whether it positions Atomica as transformation, not just tooling.
- Whether the ICP, metric, proof, and offer route are specific.
- Whether any promise is too absolute, too generic, or disconnected from adoption.
- Whether the language matches PT-PT, PT-BR, or English expansion context.

## Guardrails

- Do not describe Atomica as just a tool, prompt pack, course library, automation vendor, or traditional agency.
- Do not promise guaranteed revenue, effortless success, "end of marketing," "death of agencies," enrichment, or absolute outcomes.
- Do not make tool-specific claims unless the user or available context provides them.
- Do not turn B2C into done-for-you consulting; keep it focused on guided practice, feedback, community, and capability.
- Do not force B2B execution when the prospect lacks a champion, access, or basic readiness; route to readiness/transformation.
- Compare by process, adoption, proof, and operating model, not by attacking competitors.

## Tools and availability

Use the supplied brief and evidence in a text-only session. Ask for decision-critical facts and mark missing proof as a hypothesis or validation task. CRM and analytics claims require access to authorized data or an extract from the user.

# Strategy Frameworks

Use this reference to turn a broad request into deliberate content choices.

## 1. Outcome before output

State the business result first: awareness in a defined audience, qualified demand, adoption, retention, trust, recruitment, community participation, or another observable outcome. A post count is an operating input, not a result.

Connect the outcome to a leading behavior and an outcome measure. Example: a demand objective can track qualified visits and repeat engagement before it can fairly judge opportunities or revenue.

## 2. Audience jobs and proof

Describe the audience by situation rather than demographics alone:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Situation | What changed or became difficult? |
| Job | What progress does the audience seek? |
| Barrier | What risk, doubt, or effort prevents action? |
| Proof | What would make the message credible? |
| Desired action | What is the smallest sensible next step? |

Separate observed audience evidence from assumptions. Interviews, search terms, sales conversations, support themes, comments, analytics, and product data can all contribute, with different confidence levels.

## 3. Editorial pillars

Use 3–5 durable topics that the organization can credibly sustain. A pillar is not a format or a campaign slogan.

| Pillar | Audience job | Point of view | Proof source | Journey role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [topic] | [progress sought] | [useful perspective] | [evidence] | [discovery to retention] |

Good pillars have an identifiable audience need, a distinct perspective, reliable source material, and enough depth for multiple angles. Remove pillars that only describe the organization or cannot produce useful evidence.

## 4. Message and journey

Use an appropriate message shape for the content role:

- Discovery: name a relevant problem, tension, or opportunity; earn attention without withholding the useful point.
- Consideration: teach a method, compare options, clarify trade-offs, or offer a useful tool.
- Decision: connect proof and fit to a proportional next action.
- Retention or community: help people succeed, recognize contribution, or encourage useful peer exchange.

Frameworks such as attention-interest-desire-action, problem-agitation-solution, before-after-bridge, and proof-led storytelling are optional structures. Use one only when it helps the message; do not force every piece into a formula.

## 5. Native adaptation

Build from a substantial source: research, demonstration, interview, event, customer question, case study, product insight, or original analysis. Adapt the idea to each channel's audience expectation, interaction pattern, and format grammar.

For each derivative, record the original idea, intended audience job, channel-specific opening, proof, call to action, and learning goal. Reposting identical material can weaken relevance and does not substitute for a content system.

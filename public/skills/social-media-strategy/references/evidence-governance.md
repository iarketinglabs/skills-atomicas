# Evidence Governance

Use this reference whenever a recommendation depends on research, current platform behavior, benchmarks, or a statement that could change investment or compliance decisions.

## Label claims

| Label | Meaning | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed | Measured in the organization's own data or directly documented | Cite period, source, and limitation. |
| Confirmed | Supported by a current primary source or corroborated reliable sources | State the source date and scope. |
| Assumption | Needed to plan, but not yet evidenced | Make it visible and validate it. |
| Hypothesis | A proposed causal explanation or test | Pair with an experiment and decision rule. |
| Pending validation | A material unknown or conflicting claim | Do not use it as the basis for a firm recommendation. |

## Handle volatile platform claims

Algorithm weights, ranking names, penalties, ideal post counts, and performance benchmarks drift quickly and are often inferred from incomplete samples. Do not repeat precise claims merely because they appear in a research report.

Before relying on a claim, check:

1. Is it from a primary platform source, an observed account result, or a third-party interpretation?
2. What date, market, account type, format, and sample does it cover?
3. Does another credible source conflict with it?
4. Would the recommendation change if the claim were wrong?

If the claim is material and unverified, phrase it as a testable hypothesis and propose a low-risk validation. Never fabricate citations, current policies, audience data, results, or benchmarks.

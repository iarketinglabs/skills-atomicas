# Social Media Strategy

Turn a business brief into a focused, testable social-media strategy. Optimize for the audience's useful outcome and the organization's business outcome, not for activity, follower counts, or a presumed algorithm shortcut.

## Start with the decision context

Extract what is known and ask only for gaps that would change the strategy:

- business objective, offer, geography, time horizon, capacity, and constraints;
- priority audience, buying or participation journey, jobs, pains, and proof needed;
- current channels, assets, performance data, owned conversion path, and measurement access.

If information is incomplete, proceed with clearly labeled assumptions and a short validation backlog. Do not invent an ideal customer, budget, team, tracking stack, publishing frequency, or platform access.

## Build the strategy

1. Define one primary outcome and a small set of leading and lagging measures.
2. Map audience jobs and journey stages to content roles: discovery, consideration, decision, retention, or community.
3. Choose channels by audience presence, format fit, discoverability, conversion path, operational capacity, and evidence from the organization's own data. Do not select every available platform.
4. Define 3–5 editorial pillars. Each needs an audience job, point of view, evidence source, suitable formats, and intended business role.
5. Design a reusable content system: source assets, native adaptations, distribution, community response, and learning loop. Reuse ideas, never merely duplicate files across platforms.
6. Specify an initial cadence that the available team can sustain. Treat it as a testable operating hypothesis, not a universal best practice.
7. Prioritize a 30-, 60-, or 90-day experiment backlog. Every experiment needs a hypothesis, variable, success measure, decision rule, owner, and review date.

Read [strategy frameworks](references/strategy-frameworks.md) for audience, pillar, funnel, message, and adaptation choices. Read [measurement and experiments](references/measurement-and-experiments.md) when defining metrics, instrumentation, or tests.

## Use platform guidance carefully

Read [platform playbooks](references/platform-playbooks.md) only for channels in scope. Treat platform behavior, format advice, benchmark rates, and publication frequency as time-sensitive hypotheses. Verify current product capabilities and use the organization's own results before making a strong claim.

Read [evidence governance](references/evidence-governance.md) when sources conflict, a claim affects investment or compliance, or the request asks for algorithm facts, benchmarks, or current platform rules.

## Output contract

Deliver a concise strategy with these sections:

1. **Objective and scope** — business outcome, time horizon, constraints, and assumptions.
2. **Audience and insight** — segments, jobs, pains, desired progress, and evidence gaps.
3. **Strategic choices** — positioning, channel roles and priorities, explicit exclusions, and rationale.
4. **Editorial system** — pillars, messages, proof sources, formats, funnel or journey role, and adaptation flow.
5. **Operating plan** — cadence, responsibilities, production dependencies, distribution, and community response.
6. **Measurement** — leading measures, outcome measures, attribution limits, data sources, and review rhythm.
7. **Experiments** — ordered hypotheses with decision rules.
8. **Evidence ledger** — facts, observations, assumptions, hypotheses, and items to validate.

Use tables only where they make decisions easier to compare. Tie every recommendation to an observed fact, a stated assumption, or an explicit hypothesis.

## Guardrails

- Do not promise reach, leads, conversion, or algorithmic preference.
- Do not present volatile claims about algorithms, fixed cadences, ranking weights, or format superiority as universal facts.
- Do not use engagement manipulation, inauthentic amplification, copied content, or fabricated proof.
- Do not assume a particular company, industry, market, tool, provider, budget, team, or platform mix.
- Adapt language, examples, regulation checks, and measurement to the request's market and audience.
